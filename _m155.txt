perf(P155): narrow the int8 inner product to int16 multiply with int32 accumulation

P154 left an open limitation that I had stated myself: a hardware int8 GEMM unit
computes the (n,n) cross product and cannot express einsum("ij,ij->i"), the
row-wise inner product that the sparse gather-GEMV readout actually needs, so
int8 compute degenerates into an elementwise multiply plus a reduction. This
narrows the intermediate width instead.

Measured at 1b scale, 51962 by 128, best of five: the P154 form
(W_i32 * G_i32).sum(1) takes 21.8 ms and materialises two int32 intermediates of
25.4 MiB each. Multiplying as int16 and reducing with dtype=int32 takes 11.5 ms,
1.9 times faster, with intermediates of 12.7 MiB each. The arithmetic is safe
because each product is bounded by 127 times 127 which is 16129, below the int16
maximum of 32767, while the accumulation stays in int32, which is the community
convention. The nll is bit-identical at 10.7815.

Two faster-looking options were tried and rejected on measurement.
torch.einsum on two int8 tensors runs in 5.0 ms but its output dtype is int8, so
the sum wraps. torch.sum(W8 * G8, 1, dtype=int32) runs in 5.8 ms but W8 * G8 is
computed in int8 first and also wraps; both differ from the int32 reference.

I also tried dropping the explicit clamp and round from the activation
quantisation and had to revert both. Converting a float tensor with .to(int8) is
not saturating, it wraps: 1e6 becomes 64 instead of 127, so the clamp has to
stay. And the rounding differs, since .to(int8) rounds half away from zero while
torch.round is round-half-to-even; across 300 random groups every single group
differed. This corrects P151, where I had recorded the three rounding modes as
identical; that holds for np.rint but not for the torch cast.

What remains unresolved is that int32 accumulation still takes three passes,
multiply, reduce, then descale, and fusing them into one would need either
torch.compile or a hand-written CANN kernel. This machine has no C++ compiler,
torch.compile fails with "cl is not found", and behaviour on Ascend is unknown, so
I am not drawing a conclusion from the x86 failure. What also remains unverified
is which Ascend unit the int16 path actually occupies. Given the P86 evidence that
Index runs on the Vector unit, it is most likely the Vector unit doing a
vectorised multiply and reduction, which means the gain comes from halving the
intermediate width rather than from an int8 matrix unit. That is still a real
gain but it should not be described as an int8 operator speedup.
