docs(P156): int8 is usable for storage and multiply, never for accumulation

fhz asked whether int8 can be used at all. Splitting the question into three
parts gives measured answers for each.

Storage is already int8 for both the weights and the gathered activations, so
there is nothing to settle there. Multiplication with int32 accumulation is
something the hardware does natively through the Cube IMMA unit, and torch
exposes it as torch._int_mm; I confirmed it exists and that its result is
bit-identical to the int32 reference. Its limitation is that it only computes
(m,k) by (k,n) matrix products, so it cannot produce a row-wise inner product.
Taking the diagonal is semantically right but impractical: the whole-matrix form
took 42246 ms and allocated a (n_out, n_out) int32 tensor of 10.1 GiB, which
would exhaust memory at 1b scale, and the blocked forms that keep memory bounded
waste a factor equal to the block size, giving 33.4 ms at m=256 and 54.4 ms at
m=1024. The P155 int16 intermediate runs in 15.2 ms.

Accumulating in int8 is impossible in the arithmetic sense. The product of two
int8 values can reach 16129 against an int8 ceiling of 127, so a single product
overflows by a factor of 126; I confirmed that int8(-127) times int8(-127)
returns 1 rather than 16129. Accumulating over k=128 is worse by a factor of
16129. This is why community int8 GEMM always means int8 inputs with an int32
accumulator, which is a definition rather than a compromise.

The honest summary is that no int8 path beats the int16 intermediate on the CPU,
since the best int8 option is still 2.2 times slower. int16 wins because it is the
narrowest intermediate that works in software: wider than int8 so a product fits,
narrower than int32 so traffic halves. Notably the fp16 reference at 12.9 ms is
faster than the int16 intermediate at 15.2 ms, so the claim that int8 compute
saves time does not obviously hold for this sparse gather-GEMV shape.

A genuine int8 IMMA gain could still appear on Ascend because of the Vector versus
Cube split, and x86 numbers cannot settle that; the project rule is that platform
differences must be measured. The open item is therefore to compare the int16
intermediate against _int_mm or MatMulV3-int8 on the server using msprof, looking
at aiv_time against cube_time, and only if Cube is clearly faster is a CANN custom
kernel with blocked diagonals worth writing.
