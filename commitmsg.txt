perf(P145): add a precision switch for the gathered vector g, not for W

fhz asked about moving from fp32 to fp16. Three tensors have to be separated
first, because they behave completely differently (1b tier, real sizes):
  W, the weights, 25.7 MiB - CANNOT be demoted. P110 measured that after demotion
    the non-target-row update retention is only 26.67%, because W is
    ACCUMULATED STATE and loses precision on every read-back.
  g, the gather output, 25.7 MiB - worth trying. This is the one msprof identified
    as 81% of the readout cost.
  dp and h, 0.2 and 0.012 MiB - negligible.

The reason the same fp16 gives opposite answers is accumulation. W persists
across steps so its error compounds; g is an intermediate that is discarded the
moment it is used, so its error does not. P110's verdict therefore does not
transfer to g, though that does not make g safe either.

New switch --readout-gather-dtype with fp32, fp16 and bf16, defaulting to fp32.
The theoretical gain follows from g being bandwidth-bound: halving g to 12.9 MiB
takes the AIV time from 6.63 ms to roughly 3.3 ms and the per-step total from
8.18 ms to about 4.9 ms, which is the 5 ms target.

The measured cost, at 8192 x 64 x 128 with a 12.5% sparse activation h: the
forward output shifts by 1.6e-4 to 1.9e-4 relative, and the weight update by
2.1e-4 to 2.3e-4 relative, roughly 1800 fp32 eps. For reference, P131's einsum
non-bit-exactness is 3e-5, so this is an order of magnitude larger, while P110's
fp16 weights were outright destructive. The C4f3 gate uses a tolerance of 1e-2,
far looser than the 2.2e-4 observed, so it only catches genuinely absurd values.
Whether to enable it is fhz's call via a PPL A/B, not mine.
