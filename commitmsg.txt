perf(P139): two sync bases separate "sync overhead" from "the work being synced"

fhz ran the line profiler with --sync-every 8 (60 steps, 15.698 ms/step). The
table is short and decisive:
  learn_softmax:518      73.7882 ms   8 occurrences  self._nll_sum = None
                                                            (previous line: .item())
  forward_dev:394         5.0729 ms  60             try: (covers _staged_to_dev)
  _staged_to_dev:822      4.9447 ms  60             buf.copy_(torch.from_numpy(a))
  everything else         under 0.45 ms

Device execution is really about 9.2 ms per step, not 14.25. Line 518 fires only
8 times in 60 steps at 73.79 ms each, which is 9.84 ms per step amortised, and
since that wait is for eight steps of queued work, the device's own execution
is 73.79/8 = 9.2 ms while the synchronisation overhead on top is only about
0.6 ms. Bare operators 1 through 7 total 0.35 ms, so there is a 26x gap between
what the device is executing and what those operators account for. That gap is
the thing to chase.

Second, and new: the H2D staging copy costs 4.94 ms per step on the host. That is
a 12 KiB CPU-to-CPU copy, which should take microseconds. The only coherent
explanation is that the CPU is being blocked when writing the pinned buffer. The
Event records when the H2D transfer completes, but it does not wait for the NPU
kernels to finish consuming that buffer - an assumption baked in when the Event
was introduced. The CPU runs ahead of the device, the queue backs up, and by the
time the rotation reaches the fourth buffer it has to wait.

That second effect is a consequence of the first rather than an independent
problem: the device is slow at 9.2 ms, the CPU cannot keep up, the H2D queue
grows, and that is where the 5 ms comes from. So the fix belongs on the device
side, or in giving the CPU more room with a larger pinned pool.

The tool now distinguishes these. A new diagnostic makes the CPU do extra work
per step, simulating it running further ahead: if the _staged_to_dev time goes
DOWN, that proves it is waiting on the device rather than being slow memcpy. A
--pin-cap flag overrides the pool size, since if the hypothesis holds, growing
it is the remedy.

A lesson from my own P138 conclusion. I attributed the 14.25 ms to
"the .item() synchronisation overhead", which is wrong in a way that would have
sent the work in the wrong direction: it is really 9.2 ms of device execution
plus 5 ms of the CPU waiting on H2D. You cannot separate the cost of
synchronising from the cost of the work being synchronised, so a single basis
gives the wrong answer. The way out is to measure at two sync bases -
sync_every=1 waits every step, sync_every=8 waits once per eight - and solve the
pair.
