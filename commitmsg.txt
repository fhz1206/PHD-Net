perf(P135): Ascend segment data overturns the "gather is slow" assumption

fhz ran the kernel benchmark on Ascend at production dimensions (n_out=52,642,
n_h=3,072, k=128; per-step traffic Wi 51.4 MiB + W 51.4 MiB):
  (1) h[Wi] baseline              0.019 ms   jitter 1.7x (noise dominated)
  (2) index_select                0.025 ms   bit-identical
  (3) torch.take                  0.016 ms   bit-identical
  (4) einsum, full forward        0.143 ms   <- the P131 default
  (5) npu_gather_sparse_index     0.033 ms   bit-identical, SLOWER than baseline
  (6) aclsparseSpMM               library not found; needs a ctypes binding

On Ascend the gather takes 0.019 ms, against 10.26 ms on the x86 host - a 540x
difference. My P132/P134 reasoning that gather was the single biggest item is
simply wrong on the real hardware. The x86 1.7x win for torch.take shrinks to
1.2x and sits inside the noise, while the official operator is slower than the
baseline it would replace.

The most important number in that run is a ratio, not a measurement: the bare
operators total under 0.2 ms while the training log reports the readout at
10.99 ms - a 77x gap. So roughly 10.8 ms per step is spent somewhere the
benchmark does not cover. That, not the gather, is what needs to be found.

Candidates, now measured individually by the new section (7):
  - the addmm_ update, which touches W plus idx for read and write, about
    100 MiB per step and 7.4 ms even on this x86 CPU
  - implicit synchronisation (.item() and D2H points)
  - kernel dispatch overhead, which is exactly what P120's HBM-bw metric was
    added to detect and which has still never produced a reading
  - the per-step np.array_equal(h, cache_h) on the host, 0.005 ms here
  - H2D through the pinned buffer pool

Two mistakes in my own benchmark, both of the same kind - measuring the wrong
shape and therefore the wrong cost:
  1. The update was first written as W.sub_(dp*g, alpha=eta), which
     MATERIALISES an (n_out, k) temporary. P28 explicitly forbids that: it
     costs an extra 1.73 GiB of device traffic per step, 40% of the total. It
     is now the production form, W.addmm_(dp.reshape(-1,1), ht.reshape(1,-1)),
     a rank-1 AXPY with no temporary.
  2. The CPU-arm forward was written as W @ h, which is a shape error. It now
     branches the same way production does: einsum on NPU, mulsum on the CPU
     fallback.

Honest read on the 5 ms target: there is no room in the gather, which is about
0.17% of the 10.99 ms. The target lives in that 10.8 ms of uncovered overhead. If
it is dispatch, then TASK_QUEUE_ENABLE=2 plus torch.compile fusion is the right
direction; if it is the update's 100 MiB, the lever is reducing update traffic
rather than touching the gather. Section (7) settles which.
