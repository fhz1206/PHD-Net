perf(P143): msprof puts 89.3% of the readout in Index; cache identity made explicit

The timeline parser works now, and the answer is unambiguous. From op_summary on
Ascend, 600 steps, 12 operators:
  aclnnIndex_IndexAiCore_Index     1200 calls   7,867,902 us   89.3%  13.11 ms/step
  aclnnBatchMatMul_V2               600 calls     843,672 us    9.6%   1.41 ms/step
  aclnnAddcmul                      600 calls      51,694 us    0.6%   0.086 ms/step
  the other nine                    600 each      ~96,000 us    1.1%   ~0.16 ms/step
Total 14.68 ms per step, consistent with the 10.99 ms the training log reports.
The entire 26x gap is Index, which is the gather. P135's "bare operators total
0.35 ms" missed the real cost of the gather completely.

I made a mistake worth recording. I measured "the gather runs twice per step" and
changed code on the strength of it. The measurement was wrong: my spy counted
function entries, not executions of the gather itself. Counting only the branch
that actually gathers gives 1 call for the same h and 2 for a different h, so
the cache does work on this host. Ascend still shows 1200 Index calls for 600
steps, so either the cache does not take effect there or advanced indexing is
split into two kernels. That stays open.

What I changed is conservative and does not alter semantics. The _sp_gather
predicate now compares tensor object identity rather than an epoch counter, and
_lookup_ht aligns _cache_g_epoch when it hits. P122 fixed using the wrong gather
when the h differs, a silent numeric error; P143 concerns repeating the gather
when the h is the same. The two pull in opposite directions and both must hold.
Verified locally: one call for the same h, five for five steps, and two when the
h changes so nothing is wrongly reused.

The 655x between P135's 0.020 ms and msprof's 6556 us is still unexplained. P135
measured best-of-N over one fixed h, so the gather kept hitting L2, whereas in
production every step brings a new h. I suspect P135 measured a warm path. A new
section 9 in bench_accel_kernels.py measures cold versus hot, using a rotating
pool of 16 distinct h tensors. On x86 here the two are identical, 0.832 versus
0.759 ms, because there is no cache effect to see; it has to be run on Ascend.

If the cold number lands near 6.5 ms, then P135 was a warm-path illusion and the
remedy is to reduce how much h gets moved or to raise its reuse, not to swap
kernels. The kernel-swapping direction is already dead: the official operator is
slower and torch.take is within noise.
