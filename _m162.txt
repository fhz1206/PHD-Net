fix(P162): report the precision that was actually reached, not the one requested

The production run at 13:29 is good news and bad news. Good news first: the
readout backend line reads accel:auto on device npu, so the P161 fix worked and the
readout no longer falls back. The run finished 40,000 tokens in 6.8 minutes at
10.15 ms per token with the readout taking 4.853 ms per token on npu, 48.1 percent
of the step, down from the roughly 89 percent seen before, with zero OOV skipped.

The bad news is that this was neither the fp8 arm nor the int8 arm. A warning at
line 30 says the device does not support fp8 or anything more precise on the chain
and that it was automatically demoted to fp16, with the chain running fp8, int8,
fp16, bf16, int16, int32, fp32. So fp8 really is unavailable on the 910B, matching
the earlier probe, and int8 did not catch it either, meaning the run actually used
fp16 storage with fp16 compute. The instruction to keep fp8 storage with int8
compute cannot be realised on this device.

That exposed a genuine bug. The warning says demoted to fp16 while the summary line
still says compute precision = fp8, because train.py printed cfg.readout_dtype, the
requested value, rather than what the degradation chain landed on. Every later A/B
judgement would have believed it was running the fp8 arm. It now reads the resolved
dtype from the readout instance and prints the effective precision alongside the
requested one with an explicit note when they differ.

Why int8 did not catch it is still untested rather than concluded. The npu_quant
probes have never actually executed because the arguments were wrong in that
probe, and the int8 matmul probe likely fails for the same reason before falling
through to fp16. The next step is to probe npu_quant_matmul and
npu_weight_quant_batchmatmul on the device with correct signatures and see whether
they can build and multiply; if they can, --readout-dtype int8 becomes usable.
Otherwise the int8 line closes.
