feat(P154): fp8 storage with int8 compute by default, conversion on the CPU

fhz's instruction: when fp8 is unavailable, fall back to int8 compute with fp8
storage by default, keep the bit conversion on the CPU, and consider nogil.

Default behaviour changed accordingly. int8_compute="auto" now enables int8
compute whenever it is auto, not only when storage happens to be int8 as in P153.
Measured: with auto the storage is float8_e4m3fn and the compute is int8, giving
nll 10.7815 against 10.8376 for fp8 storage with fp16 compute.

The bit conversion lives in the new phdnet/backends/fp8_int8_convert.py and
runs as view(uint8), device to host, quantise on the CPU, back to device. The two
transfers are synchronisation points, 6.34 MiB each per step, and on Ascend they
may cost more than the conversion itself. That needs measuring with msprof.

On nogil, the measurement went the other way from what was expected and fhz chose
accordingly. At 1b scale, 51962 by 128 or 6.34 MiB of fp8, best of six: the torch
vectorised path takes 27.0 ms, the nogil kernel with a lookup table 137.5 ms, and
with raw powf 120.6 ms. Torch is 5.1 times faster because every step is SIMD
vectorised while the numba loop is scalar with no SIMD and no L2 prefetch. This
matches P123, where nogil was 7.8% slower per call. The pattern in this project is
that numba kernels pay off when the bottleneck is Python interpretation, as with
ltm_kernel at 8.07x, and lose when the bottleneck is memory traffic and vector
width, which is what a per-element numeric conversion is. So torch is the default
and the nogil kernel stays available behind _fp8_conv.

Two traps, both mine. The e4m3 exponent bias is 7, not 15, since it has four
exponent bits unlike fp16 or fp32; writing 15 made bits=0x40 decode to 0.0078
instead of 2.0 and 85% of elements disagreed. Float bit layouts cannot be
inferred by analogy. Second, matmul of (n,k) by (k,n) returns (n,n) with cross
terms, while einsum("ij,ij->i") returns (n,) as a row-wise inner product; these
are different semantics. I had used matmul, so y came out as (256,256) and every
downstream broadcast broke. It is now (W_i32 * G_i32).sum(dim=1), which keeps
int32 accumulation and the correct semantics.

That second point carries a caveat worth stating plainly: a hardware int8 GEMM
computes the (n,n) cross product and cannot do a row-wise inner product
directly, so in this sparse gather-GEMV shape "int8 compute" means int8 elementwise
multiply with int32 reduction rather than calling an int8 matrix unit. Whether
that reaches the int8 operators on the 910B is unverified and needs msprof.

Chat needs no change since it builds the same PHDWordLM class and therefore shares
one AccelReadout and one precision decision, though its fp16 checkpoint weights
get requantised on load, which is a different path from training and deserves its
own probe-PPL check.
